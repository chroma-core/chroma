# Construction costs at 100K and 500K vectors

The [new 500K comparison](../two-machine-v1/local-tests/LOCAL-TRANSFER-PACKED-NAV-500K-RESULT.md) validates reference-style local transfers, local distance counts and packed child centroids. Construction plus saving falls from 68.566 to 30.002 seconds; the retained reference takes 12.601 seconds. Insertion is 1.340 versus 0.699 seconds, balancing 19.379 versus 9.054, and balance CPU 66.54 versus 52.70 seconds. Keep the three changes. Direct local navigation, saving and balance scheduling remain the next investigations. Both machines are stopped and no further run starts automatically.

The [source-leaf claim experiment](../two-machine-v1/local-tests/SOURCE-LEAF-CLAIM-RESULT.md) removes all discarded preparations in the fixed 100K/32-worker run: 70 prepared/70 committed/zero discarded, versus 324/72/252 before. CPU falls from 51.85 to 30.70 seconds, but elapsed time rises from 14.055 to 15.449 seconds. Keep the claim and deferred-reassignment correction; transfer coordination and reservation contention lead the next queue. Both saved-state validation and focused concurrency tests pass. This is initial-owner construction, followed by physical redistribution, not distributed-construction throughput or quality parity.

Single-owner construction shows substantial avoidable overhead, but fewer calls or locks do not consistently produce a faster build. These experiments use 100,000 retained 768-dimensional vectors, one local owner and 32 configured workers on one machine. Most final trees have one internal root and roughly 70 leaves. They exercise leaf balancing and cache traffic, not selective internal-branch pruning or physical distribution.

Both arms of the paired results above pass the recorded membership and vector/query-bit checks. Adverse concurrent observations remain important: cleanup-on and the short-cadence run each record an add-missing-node event. Follow-up source analysis supports retry ordering, but the exact observed runtime retry sequence is not reproduced. Preserve that limitation rather than converting final integrity into proof of every intermediate event.

The [epoch10 assessment](../dynamic-review/100k-owner32-epoch10-assessment.json) records 39.344 million versus 32.142 million cleanup rows and 69 versus 66 splits. The [epoch11 assessment](../dynamic-review/100k-owner32-epoch11-assessment.json) records 68 versus 65 splits and 54,836 versus 53,071 reassignments. These work differences qualify the improvements. In epoch13 the long interval performs 75 splits and 43,378 scrubs versus 69 and 28,625 for short intervals. Lock spans overlap other work and must not be added as exclusive elapsed-time buckets.

## Comparison with the optimized reference

The [reference assessment](../dynamic-review/matched-reference-epoch3-assessment.json) records 16.696 native seconds, 23.484 CPU seconds and 784,924,672 bytes peak resident memory for 100K vectors with 32 workers and a 100K checkpoint. Its displayed insertion/balancing times are 0.049/13.85 seconds, versus dynamic long-cadence 4.273/50.635 seconds. Both finish with 76 leaves and 75 splits; complete current memberships and saved vector-bit digests match. Their reassignment work and intermediate visibility differ. The reference reports approximately 11.8K missing-node navigation observations.

The insertion timers include different work. The [insertion-boundary investigation](../dynamic-review/matched-insertion-gap-v1.md) attributes 0.496 seconds of nested input work and 2.297 seconds across 13 synchronous durable staging calls to the dynamic insertion interval. Reference insertion begins after vectors are loaded and defers durable publication to commit. Dynamic insertion also makes 1,568 small owner appends, whereas the reference joins and regroups worker results. Overlapping worker totals cannot supply a causal insertion ratio.

**Decision:** Carry these observations into the intended architecture. Owner-local work, reference-style grouping and split preparation are stronger candidates than another automatic cache or batching sweep. The pending [prepared experiments](prepared-experiments.md) remain unmeasured and paused until architectural relevance is resolved.

## Complete balance attribution at 100K

The [complete-balance 100K pair](../two-machine-v1/local-tests/FULL-BALANCE-OBSERVABILITY-RESULT.md) measures 324 prepared splits in the distributed implementation, of which 252 are discarded, versus 69 clustering calls and 69 committed splits in the reference. Extra clustering accounts for 15.698 of 25.780 additional CPU-seconds. Balance wall times are 14.055 versus 12.965 seconds; the reference has a long initial serial round, so the earlier threefold elapsed gap does not reproduce. Both saved indexes validate, both profiles cover the entire timed phase with zero lost samples, and both machines are API-stopped. Preventing repeated split preparation is the next priority; repeated posting compaction and local navigation copies follow. Instrumentation, different random construction work and overlapping scopes limit causal wall-time attribution.
