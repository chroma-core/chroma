# Evidence for the 100B POC argument

The current research goal remains incomplete. It requires construction-cost attribution and quality evidence for one globally evolving tree, query-cost comparisons at matched recall, and a reviewed scaling assessment for that design. The conditional acceptance in `100B-FINAL-SCOPED-ACCEPTANCE.json` covers its explicitly listed POC and assumptions; it does not establish completion of these additional requirements.

The retained multi-owner measurements below describe their own construction and query designs. Their throughput, recall and machine scaling cannot be transferred to the current globally evolving tree merely because both use owners. The accepted 16K experiment compares the ordinary reference with one and four local owners on one machine. It can establish structural pruning and produce construction and quality observations, but equal beam settings do not establish matched recall, and local owners do not establish physical distributed performance. Its fixed reused query cohort has unproven corpus disjointness. Runtime evidence is pending under `../fleet/matched-pruning-16k-v1`; source and experiment review are retained under `matched-pruning-16k-v1`.

The evidence supports a working sharded POC and a conditional scaling model. It does not constitute a 100B run. Measurements below belong to their preserved datasets, binaries, policies and hardware; new local POC features must not inherit an older throughput number without an explicit compatibility assumption.

The material next measurements are affected-owner versus unchanged-owner validation bytes/objects under the explicit fixed-input assumption; full canonical copying, all-owner cloning and record/namespace scan costs; routing/ownership metadata growth; and worst-case candidate/tie/result/provider workspace. Production wiring, node failures, save recovery, fleet changes and provider/GC engineering are excluded acceptance concerns. No new corpus, query-strategy comparison or cloud run is needed for this inventory.
